# Entrance & Hallway · Mockup 02 · Quiet Warmth

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status: proposed for review.** This is the first photorealistic design study, following a fresh assessment of the entrance and hallway. It is numbered `02` to preserve the earlier illustrated Mockup 01 and its notes. The new suggestions have not yet been approved by the user.

The proposal makes the existing stained glass, geometric tiles and period joinery the focus, then adds warmth through restrained textiles, dark timber, a small lamp and botanical art. Nothing is painted, and the existing console and mirror stay.

## 2. Design reasoning

### Let the existing house provide the ornament

The entrance already has several strong elements: tulip stained glass, intricate star-pattern tiles, mouldings, a textured mirror and clear glass pendants. Additional large patterns would compete with them. This version therefore uses texture and a small number of colours to make the space feel finished.

The existing console is a useful anchor. Its curved outline softens the geometry of the doors and tiles; dark walnut in the new coat stand and picture frames connects back to it. Aged brass relates to the existing front-door hardware, but is used sparingly.

### Give the two runners different jobs

The stair runner is the main introduction of rust. The hall runner is quieter: a flax field with a narrow rust border, rather than another strong multicoloured pattern beside the tiles. Together they soften the hall visually while keeping the original floor legible around them.

The stair runner has no exposed rods in this version. Its texture, edge binding and visible oak margins are enough detail. This is an aesthetic proposal, not a change to the user's permission for a fitted runner.

### Keep daily storage modest and realistic

The coat-stand nook is constrained and contains important stained glass. A slim timber stand with short arms is a better starting point here than a broad bentwood crown and spreading feet. The image deliberately shows only one everyday coat and a lead, so the visual trade-off is visible. It does not demonstrate capacity for a household's full coat collection.

The hamper is proposed beneath the console to clear the nook. Its fit and access must be checked rather than inferred from the generated image.

- **Main new colour:** muted clay/rust in the stair runner, with a smaller echo at the hall runner's border and the plant pot.
- **Quiet textile:** flax/oatmeal, used in the hall runner and lamp shade.
- **Timber:** dark walnut tones, relating the new coat stand and art frames to the retained console.
- **Metal:** small aged-brass accents, relating to the front-door hardware.
- **Green:** foliage, botanical art and the single illustrated olive jacket. The jacket is a styling example, not a required purchase.

## 8. Image review and limits

Both images were created with the built-in image-generation tool as photographic edits of JPEG conversions of the original HEIC photos. The source photos were not modified. Exact prompts, source references and output filenames are saved alongside these notes in `generation-prompts.json`.

Visual review found that the principal openings, star tile pattern, stained glass, console/mirror identity, stair, cupboards and black steel glazing remain recognisable and closely follow the photographs. The entrance image also lights the existing pendant bulbs and warms the room's overall appearance. That is a lighting interpretation, not a proposal to repaint or change the pendants.

The generated stair herringbone reads more pronounced than the fine texture intended in the prompt. Review that texture alongside the colour; a quieter weave remains the intended starting specification. The botanical pictures are illustrative placeholders, and their exact spacing relative to the mouldings must be set from measurements. Portions of the console, plant and art are naturally cropped by the source viewpoints.

Generated proportions are approximate. In particular, the coat stand, hanging coat, hamper and console are not a verified fit. The apparent runner lengths, textile colours under warm light and available oak margins are also not measurements or physical samples. Product details, small tile/glass details and reflections can be reconstructed by image generation; use the originals for the existing fabric and the notes for the proposed changes.

## 9. Review points and next step

Review the two views together: the level of warmth, the quiet hall runner beside the patterned tiles, the slim coat stand, the console balance and the botanical art. The proposal is ready for user feedback; it does not imply approval of these new choices.

Before turning it into a purchase specification, confirm the nook and console/hamper dimensions, door and cupboard clearances, the runner sizes, the stair measurements and the console's power provision. If the hamper does not fit, retain it and assess another location in a new iteration rather than substituting a smaller basket without agreement.

Any design changes after review receive a new iteration with their own images and notes.
