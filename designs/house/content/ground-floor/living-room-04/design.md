# Living Room · Mockup 04 · Smoky Plum & Old Gold

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status: coordinated palette proposal for review.** The user clarified that the previous rug was not a requirement. The sofa and rug can change together to introduce a new colour to this room while keeping the whole floor warm, chic, considered and visually rich.

This proposal explores **smoky plum-brown upholstery with an old-gold-and-olive rug**, using flax and camel as lighter textile notes. It builds on the older furniture direction and the user's actual objects from Living Room 02. It does not record any of those choices as approved.

Two opposite views show the coordinated scheme. Living Room 03's isolated petrol/olive/oxblood colour trials and every earlier iteration remain intact as historical explorations. The [live brief](/house/ground-floor/brief) records the clarified brief and current proposal.

## 1. The palette as a whole

| Role | Proposed colour / material | Connection to the house |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main seating colour | Smoky plum with brown/wine undertones; plain matte short-pile velvet character | Introduces a colour of its own, warmer and less purple than a bright aubergine, with a relationship to the existing red/rust accents. |
| Rug ground | Old gold / aged straw / softened ochre wool | Relates to the warm oak, brass and older furniture while providing a lighter surface beside the deep sofa. This is a wool colour, not metallic gold. |
| Rug drawing | Muted olive and tobacco foliage, quiet flax highlights | Connects to the green reception chairs, plants and timber without repeating the reception's rug design. |
| Accent cushions | One flax linen square and one olive lumbar with a fine gold stripe | Gives relief against the darker sofa with only two cushions. |
| Throw | Camel wool character, neatly placed | Adds another warm tactile material, without another strong pattern. |
| Existing furniture and objects | Older oak, darker turned timber, brass, marble, red hat/glass and mixed personal ornaments | Keeps the room rooted in the user's objects, rather than a newly matched decorative set. |

The connection between rooms comes through shared undertones, materials and small colour relationships. The sofa does not need to match the green chairs, and the rugs do not need to repeat the same pattern. No earlier rug or sofa colour is being treated as a fixed constraint.

## 2. View towards the kitchen: the sofa and rug together

**Image:** `towards-kitchen.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-04/towards-kitchen.png)

### Sofa

The principal colour is a muted smoky plum with enough brown to sit comfortably beside the warm oak, coffer and rust tones beyond. It should remain visibly coloured, without becoming bright purple, pink, scarlet or nearly black.

The sofa is a tailored straight three-seater, within roughly the previously explored 220–225 cm length and 90–95 cm depth as initial proportions. Modest low arms and short dark timber feet keep it from becoming a heavy statement piece. The precise sofa shape and size remain open; the image is not a product selection.

Plain, soft velvet is proposed for depth of colour and a different material from the green reception chairs' setting, rug wool and linen cushions. The final image received a material-only refinement because the first pass looked too patterned/embossed. The intended upholstery is **a quiet solid surface**, not paisley, damask or decorative brocade.

### Rug

The previous brick/slate diamond kilim is replaced completely for this study. The new rug has an old-gold ground, olive/tobacco stylised foliage and a broader, calmer framing border. Its Arts-and-Crafts-inspired garden character relates to the house without copying a specific named pattern or the small repeated Persian-style motifs used in earlier rooms.

The richer sofa and lighter rug are chosen together: the seating supplies the deeper colour, while the floor textile connects it to warm wood and green. The rug should feel like softly aged wool, not bright mustard, shiny gold or a vivid printed yellow field.

The pattern renders more densely and prominently in some areas than the open ground envisaged in the prompt. Review the motif scale and contrast as well as colour. A real candidate with more breathing room could keep this palette while making the surface calmer.

### Cushions and throw

Use two cushions, rather than retaining the three from the earlier colour trials: a flax square with restrained plum piping and a lower olive lumbar with a fine old-gold stripe. Keep most of the sofa visible. The camel throw is a warm texture, not an invitation to add more cushions or layers.

The original rust/olive/flax cushion arrangement is not discarded; it is simply not the proposed pairing for this palette. No new purchase is assumed where existing textiles can provide the required role.

## 4. How this aims for a more considered result

The sense of quality should come from the fabric, proportions, rug drawing and patina of individual furniture, with clear everyday surfaces. It does not depend on adding metallic decoration or buying a full set of new objects.

The sofa and rug carry the main new colour. Older timber and brass provide depth; flax and camel soften the contrast; the user's objects provide personal detail. This is a designer proposal for a coherent room, not evidence that any object shown is an expensive or genuine antique.

The reception's green chairs and the house's rust/terracotta accents remain related colours, but this room has a different dominant balance. Each room can be part of the same house without sharing one upholstery colour or one rug pattern.

## 6. Fit and material selection

Maintain a modest oak margin before the steel doors and the kitchen, with the sofa's front feet anchored on the rug. Keep the TV-side through-route usable and allow door operation. Choose the actual rug perimeter with a floor template; the image does not establish its size.

Check sofa, coffer, coffee-table and full dog-bed footprints together. The existing TV mount, socket and device/cable arrangements remain practical questions from 02, not details solved by these images.

Before any upholstery selection, test real plum samples beside the rug colours, oak and existing objects in daylight and evening light. A colour that reads warm brown-plum here may look different in the room. Velvet nap, cleaning performance, durability and the rug's actual weave are not established by a generated image. No particular fabric performance is being claimed.
