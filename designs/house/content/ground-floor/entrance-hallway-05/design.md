## 1. What this design is doing

The V1 brief gives the entrance its own identity: an Aesthetic hall whose stained-glass tulip door and geometric floor tiles remain the main features. Black bentwood and black picture frames supply a crisp line; one blue-and-white lamp supplies porcelain detail; terracotta appears in a plant pot and the hallway textile.

This replaces the earlier repeated formula of pleated brass lamps, gilt landscape paintings and floral rugs. The new runner retains the user's requested brown Persian character through angular Heriz/Serapi-type drawing, with faded contrast to sit beside the much sharper tiles. The console remains deliberately restrained, with exactly three items.

The coat on the newel, cap on the stair and grey storage box at the lower left are removed. Oak treads and risers remain bare. White balusters and newel, dark handrail, panelling, cupboards, black steel doors, rear doorway/fanlight and the kitchen/garden view remain.

### Runner

The runner has a faded terracotta-brown field with camel and softened charcoal drawing: angular central medallions, angular corner motifs and a narrow geometric border. It has short or no fringe. Its angular language follows the brief's response to the floor tiles; the antique-washed appearance reduces competition between the two patterns.

The brief's starting size is **65–70 cm × about 300 cm**, with tiles visible on both sides. It calls for a start about **30 cm past the stair foot** and an end about **30 cm before the rear threshold**, leaving the entrance square uncovered. The image shows uncovered tile at both ends, but those gaps and the rug's length are indicative rather than dimensionally verified. The pattern remains visible; it has not been erased into a plain rug.

The first pass was too strongly outlined. A second, runner-only edit softened the motifs and border and increased the uncovered end bands. The first pass is retained in `process/hallway-first-pass.png` for traceability.

### Stair prints

Three portrait-format Japanese bird-and-flower woodblock-style images rise diagonally above the panelling: a bird on a flowering branch, a kingfisher with irises and a heron in snow. Each has a narrow black frame and a wide ivory mount. These are conceptual ukiyo-e-style placeholders, not identified or sourced artworks.

The brief proposes **about 45 × 60 cm framed**, with bottom edges **about 20–25 cm above the panelling's top rail** and even spacing. All three are shown above the panels, without picture lights. Their apparent size and clearances require a measured hanging layout. If the user has preferred existing art, it takes priority under V1.

## 5. Measurements and practical checks from V1

Before selecting products, measure:

- The coat-stand nook's clear width and depth, approximately 0.5 × 0.5 m on the drawing; check the chosen stand's actual footprint and hanging-coat projection.
- The clear height and depth beneath the console against the existing hamper. Do not reduce the hamper to make the scheme fit.
- The clear hall width beside the stair, approximately 0.95 m on the drawing, and the runner route from stair foot to rear threshold. Preserve cupboard and doorway access.
- The actual upper stair-wall height and frame positions above the sloping panelling before ordering or fixing the three prints.

The drawing shows a socket on the console wall. Verify it; if absent, V1 specifies a cordless rechargeable lamp. Use a suitable non-slip runner underlay compatible with the tile floor and any underfloor heating. All new pieces remain **unsourced concepts**, not product selections or purchase recommendations.
