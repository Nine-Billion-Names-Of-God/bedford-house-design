# Entrance & Hallway · Mockup 04 · Softly Faded

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status: revised proposal for review.** The user found Mockup 03's console too crowded and its Persian-style runner too strongly patterned beside the existing geometric tiles. This version edits the console down to three pieces and explores a softer brown rug with a much more faded traditional design.

The direction is **curated, elegant and chic**, expressed through considered proportions, visible tabletop and quiet tonal relationships. Bare oak stairs, traditional landscapes above the panelling, existing architecture, paint, console and mirror remain part of the scheme. Earlier iterations are preserved unchanged.

## 3. Hallway view: a softer Persian-style runner

**Image:** `hallway-view.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/entrance-hallway-04/hallway-view.png)

The revised runner has a light-to-medium **mushroom, warm taupe-brown and pale tobacco** ground. Its Persian-style floral drawing and small medallions remain discernible, but are much less distinct than in 03. Border and field sit close in tone, with no strong dark perimeter or bright ivory outlines.

The intended effect is a rug whose design seems worn into the wool: some drawing survives, other areas nearly dissolve. The material should still feel tactile and clearly photographed. “Blurry” describes the faded pattern, not poor image focus or a physically damaged rug that must be fragile.

### Why this works better with the floor

The original star tiles already provide crisp geometry and pronounced light/dark contrast. The earlier rug introduced a second strong pattern with a dark frame around it. Here, both patterns remain, but the rug contributes a softer brown surface while the tiles retain the sharper drawing.

Lightening and desaturating the brown relates it to the oak without introducing a bright orange or red stripe. The border is deliberately quiet so it does not form another rigid frame within the narrow hall. Continuous tile margins remain visible at the sides.

The far end is also pulled back from the rear doorway, revealing a band of patterned tile before the kitchen threshold. This gives the rug a clearer endpoint. Its near end is outside the image crop, so the starting position still needs to be set in the actual hall.

Bare oak treads and risers remain exposed. The three traditional landscapes and aged gilt frames remain above the stair panelling in the diagonal arrangement from 03. No additional artwork, picture lights or floor furniture are introduced in this revision.
