# Ground Floor Redesign — Live Brief

This is the living record for the ground floor redesign at 92 Woodstock Road. It captures inputs, constraints, decisions and open questions as the conversation progresses, so nothing lives only in chat history. It is updated as we go; design iterations themselves live in separate versioned files.

---

## 1. The house

- **Address:** 92 Woodstock Road, Bedford Park, Chiswick, London W4.
- **Type:** Victorian house in Bedford Park.
- **Context note:** Bedford Park is a late-Victorian garden suburb (built from 1875), largely Queen Anne Revival / Arts and Crafts in character, and a conservation area with many listed houses. The design should respect that character. Whether this specific house is listed is an open question (see §9), as it affects what can physically change.
- **Recent history:** the ground floor was substantially renovated to the architect's design (tender issued April 2024). Floors, glazed screens, kitchen, hall tiling, mouldings and the cloakroom are all recent.

## 2. Scope

All of the following ground-floor areas are in scope:

| Area | Notes |
|---|---|
| Entrance | Front door and the tiled entrance zone. |
| Hallway | Runs from the entrance past the stairs to a door into the kitchen. |
| Cloakroom under the stairs | WC and basin under the stair slope, off the hall. |
| Front lounge | Dual use: home office **and** the main lounge. Bay-windowed front room. |
| TV area | Middle zone between the front lounge's glazed screen and the kitchen; open to the kitchen. |
| Kitchen | Rear open-plan extension: kitchen, island, dining table and the seating by the garden doors. |

**Nature of the change: decor-led.** No painting anywhere on the ground floor; wall, ceiling and joinery colours stay exactly as they are. The design works through furniture, textiles (rugs, runners, upholstery, curtains, cushions), lighting, plants, art and accessories.

## 6. Working agreements

1. **Philosophy first.** We agree the problem framing and design philosophy before any design work starts.
2. **Never edit an iteration in place.** Every design change is saved as a new versioned iteration (e.g. `01`, `01b`, `02`) so the full history stays visible.
3. **This brief is live.** New inputs, decisions and answers are recorded here as they happen, with a dated entry in the decision log.

## 7. Design philosophy

**Status: agreed (2026-10-25).**

1. **Two deliberate characters.** The front of the house (entrance, hallway, cloakroom, front lounge) is period: lean into the Bedford Park / Arts and Crafts features rather than neutralising them. The rear (kitchen, dining, garden end) is contemporary. The TV area is the hinge between the two and borrows from both.
2. **A connecting thread.** A shared palette and set of materials runs through every area so the two characters feel like one house. Terracotta / rust is the main connecting colour; it already appears in the cloakroom, the bar stools and cushions. Because nothing is painted, the thread is carried by textiles, upholstery, ceramics, pots, art and accessories.
3. **Good decor everywhere.** Every area should feel finished and considered, not just functional.
4. **Room by room.** We agree what to keep, change, add and remove for each area before any design work, starting at the front door and working back.

## 8. Room by room

### 8.1 Entrance and hallway

**Status: v2 agreed (2026-10-25). Mockup 01 drawn (`entrance-hallway-01`), awaiting review.** Decor-led: no painting, and the console stays.

- **Keep as is:** front door, stained glass, fanlight and side light (the hero of the hall); geometric floor tiles; wall colours and panelling; cornice and the mid-hall arch; ribbed-glass pendant cluster; stair (oak treads, white balusters, dark handrail, newel); white panelled doors; the clear sightline from the front door through to the garden; the hall cupboards (shoes, umbrellas, dog things); the demi-lune console and mirror; the Fortnum & Mason hamper for dog things (moved under the console in mockup 01; see the nook note below).
- **Change:**
  - **Coat pole → bentwood hat-and-coat stand** in a dark finish to match the console. Bentwood stands are of the same era as Bedford Park, echo the bentwood armchairs in the front lounge, and need no fixings. Stand it towards the corner so it does not sit in front of the middle of the stained-glass side light; one or two everyday coats per person, with the dog lead on a low hook. Alternative if the bentwood base is too wide for the nook: a slim dark-timber stand with aged-brass hooks.
  - **Nook note (from re-measuring the drawing):** the nook is only about 0.5 m wide × 0.5 m deep, and the side light fills most of its back wall. The stand and the hamper can't both fit, so mockup 01 puts the hamper under the console. Coats on the stand will cover the lower part of the side light, so keep it to two or three. Measure on site.
  - **Console styling, edited to three things:** a lamp at one end, a plant at the other, and one tray or dish for keys under the mirror. The clock and loose boxes find another home.
  - **Doormat → larger coir mat**, plain or with a slim border, the full width of the door, to catch dog paws.
- **Add:**
  - **Hall floor runner** in the narrow stretch from the foot of the stair to the rear hall door, not in the entrance square by the door, where the tiles make the first impression. Flatweave or low pile, vintage or vintage-style, in faded rust/terracotta with ink-blue and green. About 60 cm wide so a band of tile shows on both sides (measure the clear width). Non-slip underlay for the tiles and the dog; low tog value for the underfloor heating (flatweave is ideal).
  - **Stair runner** (carried over from v1): wool, plain or subtle herringbone, in terracotta / rust with a narrow border and aged-brass rods. Kept plain so it does not compete with the patterned floor runner beside it. With no paint, the two runners are how colour enters the hall. Needs fitting to the oak treads (see §9).
  - **Plant on the console:** an aspidistra (cast-iron plant), the classic Victorian hall plant. Copes with low light and front-door draughts, and is non-toxic to dogs. In a terracotta or glazed pot.
  - **Warm lamp on the console:** a small aged-brass or ceramic lamp with a fabric shade and a warm, dimmable bulb, for a glow in the evenings. The drawing shows a socket on this wall; if it is not there as built, use a cordless rechargeable lamp.
  - **Art up the stair:** two or three pieces hung within the existing panels, one frame finish (picture hooks agreed).
- **Remove:** the white coat pole, coats on the newel post, the towel by the door, and the clutter on the console.
- **Hardware rule (proposed):** aged brass for new decorative metal at the front of the house, matching the front door's existing brass.
- **Iterations:** `entrance-hallway-01`: two illustrated views (entrance looking to the front door; hallway looking to the garden).

*Superseded v1 (2026-10-25):* repaint the walls (warm off-white over deep green below the dado) and replace the console with a storage bench. Rejected: no painting anywhere, and the console stays.

2. **Piano** — staying, presumably; is its current position fixed, or can it move?
3. **Extent of change** — confirmed: no painting anywhere (2026-10-25). Assumed: the recently renovated fabric also stays (kitchen units and worktops, flooring, hall tiles, glazed steel screens, alcove joinery, mouldings, cloakroom fit-out), and the redesign covers layout, furniture, textiles, lighting, plants, art and accessories.
4. **Dining bench and chairs** — fixed with the new table, or open?
5. **Dog** — the raised pet bed and bowl suggest a dog. Where should its bed and food live? (Lead and outdoor kit: hall cupboards, hamper and a low hook on the coat stand.)

7. **Entrance measurements to take on site**: width and depth of the coat-stand nook (about 0.5 × 0.5 m on the drawing); clear depth under the console and the hamper's size; clear hall width beside the stair; length from the foot of the stair to the rear hall door; whether there is a socket by the console.
