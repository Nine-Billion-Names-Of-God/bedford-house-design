## 1. The house

- **Address:** 92 Woodstock Road, Bedford Park, Chiswick, London W4.
- **Type:** Victorian house in Bedford Park.
- **Context note:** Bedford Park is a late-Victorian garden suburb (built from 1875), largely Queen Anne Revival / Arts and Crafts in character, and a conservation area with many listed houses. The design should respect that character. Whether this specific house is listed is an open question (see §9), as it affects what can physically change.
- **Recent history:** the ground floor was substantially renovated to the architect's design (tender issued April 2024). Floors, glazed screens, kitchen, hall tiling, mouldings and the cloakroom are all recent.

## 6. Working agreements

1. **Philosophy first.** We agree the problem framing and design philosophy before any design work starts.
2. **Never edit an iteration in place.** Every design change is saved as a new versioned iteration (e.g. `01`, `01b`, `02`) so the full history stays visible. Preserve both the previous images and their accompanying design notes; revisions receive a new iteration folder rather than overwriting earlier files.
3. **This brief is live.** Record the key discussion points, user preferences, constraints, decisions, reasons, open questions and current next step here, with dated entries in the decision log. This is a concise handover record for the whole ground-floor project, not a verbatim transcript. Keep confirmed instructions distinct from design proposals and unverified assumptions.
4. **Every mockup has detailed design notes.** Each iteration must be understandable on its own: include the design intent and reasoning, reference inputs, constraints, what stays/changes/is added/is removed, item placement and suggested dimensions, materials and palette, practical trade-offs, assumptions and measurements to confirm, and review status. Explain each image separately within the notes, so every view has a clear account of what it shows. For later iterations, explain the changes from the previous version and why. These notes are separate from the live brief.
5. **Publish notes with the images using the existing repository convention.** Follow the shed's paired-folder structure: `designs/house/content/ground-floor/<iteration>/design.md` for the website-readable design notes, and `designs/house/assets/ground-floor/<iteration>/` for the corresponding images. The shared iteration slug associates the notes with their companion gallery. Use Markdown for the detailed narrative, as the existing website renders Markdown documents; JSON metadata is optional for labels and ordering, not a substitute for the design notes. Record the image filenames and their view descriptions in the notes. Save generation prompts in the iteration's notes or an accompanying referenced file for reproducibility.
6. **Use photorealistic visuals for the room mockups.** Use image generation with the current house photos as references, preserving the actual architecture, paint, floors and retained furniture. The earlier simplified drawings remain historical iterations. Generated images are design studies, not confirmation of exact dimensions or product fit; record material deviations from the references in the design notes.
7. **Design direction comes from versioned image briefs (from 2026-10-25).**
   - **Who owns what:** Copilot owns the design. ChatGPT is the image tool and adds no styling of its own. The user reviews the images and gives feedback to Copilot.
   - **The briefs:**
     - `image-briefs/house-direction/` holds the shared house story, room identities, rules and the formula to avoid.
     - `image-briefs/<room>/` holds each room's specification and paste-ready prompts.
     - Each brief is versioned separately (`v1.md`, `v2.md`…), so rooms can move at their own pace.
     - A version is never edited once handed over.
   - **Generating:** ChatGPT edits the **original photos**, one room per session, and saves the result as the next iteration.
   - **Records:** each iteration's `design.md` names the room-brief and House Direction versions it implements.
8. **Moving on is not approval.** Moving to another room, or asking for changes between iterations, does not mean a mockup is agreed. Only an explicit confirmation counts.

## 7. Design philosophy

**Status: agreed (2026-10-25).**

1. **Two deliberate characters.** The front of the house (entrance, hallway, cloakroom, front lounge) is period: lean into the Bedford Park / Arts and Crafts features rather than neutralising them. The rear (kitchen, dining, garden end) is contemporary. The TV area is the hinge between the two and borrows from both.
2. **A connecting thread.** A shared palette and set of materials runs through every area so the two characters feel like one house. Terracotta / rust is a connecting colour already present in the cloakroom, bar stools and cushions. It need not be the dominant colour in every room: the user welcomes new upholstery/rug colours designed together, provided the whole floor remains warm-toned, chic and considered. Because nothing is painted, the connections are carried by textiles, upholstery, ceramics, pots, art and accessories.
3. **Good decor everywhere.** Every area should feel finished and considered, not just functional.
4. **Room by room.** We agree what to keep, change, add and remove for each area before any design work, starting at the front door and working back.
5. **Related rooms, distinct identities.** User feedback (2026-10-25): repeated similar rugs, walnut pieces and generic decor made the rooms too alike. Develop more individual, older/collected character and interesting pieces while keeping the shared palette/material thread. Inspect and work with the user's actual ornaments and useful existing pieces; do not replace them with generic pottery or a repeated styling formula in pursuit of restraint. Curated does not mean removing personal character.
