## 6. How to style the shelves without filling them

The important change is variety of shape and material, not replacing rows of books with rows of ornaments. Begin by choosing the meaningful objects already owned, then leave room around them.

| Shelf role | Practical arrangement | Balance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upper display | One plate or shallow bowl with a small book group nearby | Do not put a large patterned object on every upper shelf. |
| Main eye-level display | One small framed picture and a low bowl or box, with clear space between | Keep frames small enough to sit within the existing shelf heights. |
| Lower display | One stronger ceramic form beside useful books | A single vase can carry more presence than several tiny ornaments. |
| Base cupboards | Concealed everyday storage | Retain convenient cupboard access; loose work items do not need to be on show. |

A useful starting balance is roughly one-third books, one-third selected objects/groups and one-third open space, adjusted to what is actually owned. It is a guide rather than a quota. A in the render retains more books; B more clearly demonstrates the mixed-object direction requested.

Avoid buying several matching vases, stripping useful books out for their spine colour or placing an ornament in every gap. Personal photographs and inherited objects can replace the illustrated placeholders. The note describes the intended composition; the images do not establish an inventory of possessions.

## 7. Painting proportions and placement

| Position | Starting proportion | Selection and fitting considerations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Over the fireplace | A horizontal work around the current framed print's overall width; an initial comparison around 70–80 × 45–55 cm overall | Fit within the actual chimney-breast mouldings, with room around the frame. Do not enlarge or repaint the panel. |
| Above the piano | A modest work around 55–65 × 40–50 cm overall as a starting template | The generated A picture reads taller than this; measure the available wall and keep the frame clear of mouldings and door movement. |
| On a shelf | A small frame, often around 15–25 cm overall if it fits | Respect existing shelf height and use a stable placement; do not add shelves to fit a picture. |

These are proportional ideas, not an instruction to purchase those exact sizes. Try paper templates before selecting frames. The artwork shown is generated to communicate subject and palette; no artist, original painting, antique authenticity or available product is represented.

The original architectural picture over the fireplace remains a valid alternative. It could work with either cushion and shelf direction if retaining it is preferred. This exploration does not authorise discarding or relocating it without agreement.
