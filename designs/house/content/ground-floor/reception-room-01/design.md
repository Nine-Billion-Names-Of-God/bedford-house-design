# Reception Room · Mockup 01 · A Considered Study

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status: first reception-room proposal, for review.** The user asked to develop the front room with the green armchairs, piano and study: introduce a rug, style the shelves and assess the furniture arrangement, without painting walls or changing the recently renovated fabric.

The proposal gives the armchairs a defined setting beside the fireplace, keeps the working desk in the bay, retains the piano on its current wall and clears the middle of the room. The atmosphere is traditional, warm and considered, following the user's preference for a curated, elegant room rather than layers of accessories.

This is a new room iteration. All entrance/hallway mockups and their notes remain unchanged. The [live brief](/house/ground-floor/brief) records the wider project; these notes explain this particular design.

## 2. Layout and reasoning

### Make the seating a room within the room

The armchairs currently sit beside the fireplace, but the broad area of unbroken floor and the mobile pedestal in the middle make their relationship less clear. Retain one chair on each side of the hearth, bring them slightly away from the shelving and angle them inward. A rug under their front feet and one small round table give them a shared centre.

The round table has no corners projecting into the route and is much smaller than a full coffee table. Keep its top almost empty: one small coaster/dish is enough. Each chair receives one cushion, with oatmeal on the nearer chair and dusty rust on the bay-side chair.

### Keep the study genuinely usable

The desk remains in the bay for this version. Moving it to the piano wall would displace the instrument or introduce another substantial piece into the circulation route. Retaining the bay position lets this first proposal improve the room without adding joinery or replacing useful furniture.

The two monitors and office chair stay visible and functional. Tidy cables into a discreet under-desk route rather than disguising the study as a decorative writing table. The mobile oak pedestal moves from mid-room to the right end of the desk, towards the piano, subject to its actual fit and the desk's full lift movement. It must not occupy knee space or the chair's roll area.

### Preserve a clear route to the piano and desk

Keep an exposed oak route on the right between the rug and the piano. The rug belongs to the chair group, rather than covering the entire floor. Its edge must sit outside the office chair's working roll path. Leave room to pull out and use the piano bench, open the piano as required and operate the glazed doors.

The images suggest these relationships; confirm them in the real room with the desk chair rolled back, the piano bench occupied and the door leaves open.

## 3. Reception view: from the hall doorway

**Image:** `reception-view.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/reception-room-01/reception-view.png)

This view shows the seating group against the existing fireplace and shelving. Both green chairs keep their actual design and scale. The small walnut table is the centre of the group; the rug links the pieces while leaving much of the oak floor visible.

The rug is a low-pile traditional/Persian-style design in warm stone and tobacco-brown, with muted moss and dusty-rust detail. It is richer than the softly faded hall runner because the reception has a plain timber floor rather than patterned tiles, but still avoids a bright red field or a stark black border. It should remain a supporting surface for the green chairs, not the room's loudest element.

The alcoves have more deliberate book groupings, selected frames and a few ceramics. The mantel keeps the existing architectural print, with one olive vase and a small brass candlestick beneath it. The centre stays clear. The three small plant pots previously on the mantel are proposed for redistribution elsewhere in the house, not disposal.

A linen-shaded brass reading lamp behind the bay-side armchair provides light for that seat and a warmer evening atmosphere. The shelving is retained in its existing colour and configuration.

The portrait camera naturally obscures part of the piano with the existing glazed door. The piano has not been removed or relocated; it is more clearly visible in the second image.

This wider view explains how the lounge, desk and piano coexist. The seating stays to the left, the desk uses the bay and the piano remains to the right. The mobile pedestal is no longer a freestanding object between the chairs and workstation.

## 5. Shelf styling plan

The aim is a real, edited home library, not a shelf full of decorative purchases. Begin with the user's actual books, meaningful frames and objects. The illustrated book bindings and pictured objects are placeholders rather than an inventory of possessions.

| Shelf area | Starting arrangement | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upper shelves | Small groups of upright books or one shallow bowl, with visible gaps | Filling every space, oversized plants, a repeated ornament on every shelf |
| Middle shelves | Books in groups, one modest frame leaning against the back, a single ceramic vessel where space permits | Several unrelated frames competing side by side, covering books with ornaments |
| Lower open shelves | More of the usable books, mostly upright, with an occasional low horizontal stack | Tall decorative piles, excess objects that make retrieving books awkward |
| Base cupboards | Loose papers, work accessories and small miscellaneous items that do not need display | Leaving routine storage scattered across open shelves and the floor |
| Mantel | Existing wide architectural print; one modest vase at one end and a small unlit candlestick at the other | A row of plant pots, a dense mantel display or extra small pictures around the existing print |

Use varied book heights and ordinary as well as clothbound volumes. Brown, muted green, cream and burgundy relate well to the room, but do not replace good books just to achieve a colour scheme. Grouping and open space matter more than matching spines.

Across the alcoves, begin with only a few meaningful frames and two or three ceramics in total. The renders vary slightly in exact shelf contents. Use this styling plan as the guide and assess the actual possessions before deciding what stays on display.

## 7. What is added, moved and retained

- **Added / proposed:** one seating rug, one small round table, two cushions, reading lamp, task lamp and optional outer linen curtain panels; a small number of display pieces if suitable ones are not already owned.
- **Moved / reorganised:** armchairs slightly forward and inward; pedestal to the desk edge; books and personal objects into calmer groups; mantel plant pots and hearth basket to other appropriate storage/display locations.
- **Retained:** green chairs, piano and bench, desk, monitors and task chair for this study, architectural print, chandelier, sheers, all original paint and joinery, fireplace, floor and black steel glazing.
- **Cleared:** bags, loose clothing, cable tangles and miscellaneous small objects from the main floor and display surfaces. This is proposed tidying and redistribution, not authorisation to dispose of possessions.

No extra sofa, bench, new storage cabinetry, wall repainting or structural work is part of this version.

Visual review confirms the two green chairs, piano, bay study with two monitors, rug-defined seating zone, cleared centre, styled shelves and retained period architecture. The renders are warmer in illumination than the original photos; this is a daylight/lamp treatment, not a paint proposal. Small shelf inventories, rug drawing, fabric detail, furniture proportions and reflections differ between the two generated views. They express one design direction rather than a photogrammetric model.

The room's actual desk/chair roll area, piano bench clearance, cabinet access, curtain provision, hearth distances and furniture fit remain to be measured. The rug can read stronger in the images than the subdued concept; assess a real sample alongside the chairs and flooring before selecting it.

Review the rug's tone and pattern, the chair/table relationship, the shelf density and whether keeping the desk in the bay works for daily use. The user has authorised this proposal, but has not yet approved the new furniture, rug or styling choices.
