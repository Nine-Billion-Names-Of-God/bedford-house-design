## 1. Design intent: the Aesthetic hall

The stained-glass tulip door and the grey, black and white geometric tiles are already the hero. The hall has plenty of pattern and colour. What it needs is **a few crisp, graphic, period pieces** and one warm textile, not more busy decoration.

The look is an 1880s Bedford Park hall belonging to collectors:

- **Black (ebonised) and aged brass:** the coat stand, the frames, the brass that already appears on the door furniture.
- **Blue and white:** one piece of porcelain.
- **Three Japanese woodblock prints up the stair:** Japonisme was the art of the Aesthetic Movement and of Bedford Park itself.
- **One warm runner** that carries the house's terracotta thread to the front door. It also links with the terracotta cloakroom, which opens off this hall.

**Earlier user preferences this design keeps:**
- wall colour, console and mirror unchanged
- the F&M hamper still holds the dog things
- hall cupboards for shoes, umbrellas and dog things
- the coat pole replaced
- a runner on the floor and a plant on the console
- bare oak stairs (no stair runner)
- three traditional, not modern, pictures rising diagonally above the stair panelling
- a curated, chic console with very few things
- a brown Persian-character runner that must not clash with the tiles

**Why the earlier runners clashed, and the fix.** The curvy, floral pattern fought the crisp geometric tiles, and its contrast was too high. This runner uses a **geometric** Persian design: Heriz/Serapi-type angular medallions and corners, which share the tiles' angular language. It is **antique-washed**, so the pattern is low contrast. The field is **warm terracotta-brown**, which sits well against grey, black and white instead of disappearing into mushroom beige.

## 7. Notes to record in `design.md`

- **Measure on site:**
  - the nook's width and depth (about 0.5 × 0.5 m on the drawing);
  - the clear height and depth under the console for the hamper;
  - the hall's clear width beside the stair (about 0.95 m on the drawing), which sets the runner width;
  - the length from the foot of the stair to the rear threshold;
  - the wall height above the stair panelling, for the prints.
- **Lamp:** the drawing shows a socket on the console wall. If there isn't one, use a cordless rechargeable lamp.
- **Prints:** these are placeholders for real prints. Good-quality later reprints or genuine antique ukiyo-e prints are both available. If the user owns art they would rather hang here, it takes priority.
- **Runner:** use non-slip underlay suited to tiles and underfloor heating.
- **Concepts only:** all new pieces are concepts, not sourced products.
