# House Direction · V1

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status:** design direction for the entrance and hallway, the reception room and the living room (TV area). The kitchen, dining and cloakroom will be added in later versions.

**Who owns what:**
- **Copilot** owns the design: the room identities, layouts, item choices and instructions.
- **ChatGPT** turns the briefs into photorealistic images. It does not add its own styling.
- **The user** reviews the images and gives feedback to Copilot, who issues the next brief version.

**Versioning:** this file is never edited after handover; changes become `v2.md`. Each room has its own versioned brief in `image-briefs/<room>/`.

## 0. For the image generator: how to use this

1. Read this document first, then the room brief. The room brief gives the exact specification and prompts; this document gives the rules every room shares. If they ever disagree, the room brief wins for that room.
2. Generate one room per session.
3. If something is not specified, leave it as it is in the original photo. Do not fill gaps with your own decor.

## 1. Why we are resetting

The user has confirmed that **none of the earlier iterations were agreed**: entrance-hallway 01–04, reception-room 01–04 and living-room 01–02. They were explorations. Each room is therefore redesigned from scratch, and earlier mockups stay in the repository as history only.

Every earlier iteration repeated one formula:

- **Rugs:** Persian-style floral rugs (hall, reception) and a kilim (living room).
- **Art:** gilt-framed landscape and still-life oils (stair, mantel, piano wall).
- **Lamps:** brass with pleated or linen shades in every room.
- **Furniture:** round walnut pedestal tables and turned-leg antique pieces.
- **Textiles:** the same cushion trio (rust velvet, olive, striped flax lumbar) and a cream fringed throw.
- **Light:** heavy golden-hour colour grading that made rooms darker and browner than they are.

The result was three rooms that read as one generic English country-house hotel. Worse, **the household's own things were edited out**:

- the long skyline drawing over the mantel was replaced by a gilt oil painting;
- family photographs on the alcove shelves were replaced by props;
- shelf ornaments were replaced by generic pottery.

## 2. The house story: a Bedford Park house of collectors

Bedford Park (from 1875) was the first garden suburb, built around Norman Shaw's houses for artists and followers of the Aesthetic Movement. Aesthetic households were **collectors**. They hung Japanese woodblock prints, displayed blue-and-white and Imari porcelain, bought Eastern textiles from Liberty, and used ebonised (black) furniture. William Morris's rugs were woven at Kelmscott House in Hammersmith, two miles away.

This household already collects in the same spirit:

- the miniature paintings and the hand-painted vessel basin in the cloakroom
- the Vietnamese hat
- the figures and objects on the living-room shelf
- the carved mask in the dining area
- the skyline drawing in the reception
- the tulip stained glass on the front door

**The idea:** the house's personal objects are the decoration, and new pieces are the setting for them. The period is expressed as the Aesthetic Movement of the 1880s, which was itself a collector's style, not as a generic country house.

The agreed philosophy stays, with the period front, the TV area as the hinge and the contemporary rear:

| Zone | Era | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Hall and reception | 1880s Bedford Park, the Aesthetic Movement | Collected, crisp, period |
| TV area | Mid-century (about 1955–75) | The hinge between the two |
| Kitchen and dining | Today | Contemporary, as built |

## 4. Rules for every room

**Never change:**
- **No painting anywhere.** Wall, ceiling and joinery colours stay exactly as in the photos.
- **Architecture:** mouldings, cornices, panelling, floors, tiles, doors, stained glass, black steel glazing and fireplaces.
- **Fixed furniture:**
  - the hall console and mirror
  - the Fortnum & Mason hamper
  - the green velvet armchairs
  - the piano and bench
  - the desk, monitors and task chair
  - the dining table, bench and chairs
  - the bouclé tub chairs and their marble side tables
  - the hall cupboards

**Keep the user's own things.** Keep the user's objects, art, photos and plants wherever the room brief says so. Never replace them with props.

**Only what is listed.** Add only the items each room brief lists. No extra:
- candles or flowers
- trays or stacked books
- vases or bowls
- trailing plants
- throws or extra cushions
- art

**The dog.** Show the real raised grey dog bed where specified; never shrink, restyle or hide it. Rugs use non-slip underlay suited to underfloor heating.

**Daylight.** Use natural daylight consistent with the source photos. No golden-hour grading, no darkening, no added haze.

## 5. The formula to avoid

Do not use any of the following anywhere, unless a room brief explicitly specifies it:

- gilt-framed landscape or still-life oil paintings
- Persian or oriental floral rugs, and kilims. The hall runner is the one specified exception: geometric, faded and terracotta-brown.
- pleated lampshades; brass lamps with linen shades
- round walnut pedestal tables; turned-leg or barley-twist coffee tables
- rust velvet, olive or striped flax lumbar cushions; cream fringed throws
- generic pottery, matching ceramic sets, decorative boxes as props
- replacing the user's own objects, art or photos

## 6. How to generate

- **Edit target:** the **original photos** in `assets/ground-floor/images/` at the repository root (HEIC; convert to JPEG as needed). Do **not** edit or attach earlier mockups: they carry the formula. The only exception is where a room brief allows an earlier mockup as a framing reference.
- **Camera:** keep the camera position, lens and framing of the source photo. Remove people, clutter and the items each brief lists.
- **Photorealism:** materials should look real. The rendered scale of new items must follow the dimensions in the brief.
- **Saving:**
  - Save the result as the next iteration, using the folder each room brief names. Put the design notes in `designs/house/content/ground-floor/<iteration>/` and the images in `designs/house/assets/ground-floor/<iteration>/`.
  - Write `design.md` stating which room-brief version and which House Direction version it implements, with a short note per image.
  - Save `generation-prompts.json` with the exact prompts and inputs. Keep first passes in `process/`.
- **Checks:** before saving, go through the room brief's checklist. List in `design.md` anything the image could not achieve. Never drop an instruction silently.
- **History:** never modify earlier iterations.
- **Feedback:** design feedback goes to Copilot. Do not reinterpret feedback into new styling yourself; wait for the next brief version.
