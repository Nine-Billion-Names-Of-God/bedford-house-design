## 1. Design intent: the library and music room

This is the best room in the house: a crystal chandelier, a panelled ceiling, a canted bay, a white marble-style fireplace and two alcoves of shelving. It is used three ways: reading by the fire, playing the piano and working at the desk in the bay. The design gives it the character of an **1880s Bedford Park library and music room**. The best references are William Morris's Kelmscott House, two miles away in Hammersmith, where his rugs were woven, and the Aesthetic Movement's taste for collected porcelain.

**The hero is the fireplace wall.** It holds the user's own long panoramic pen-and-ink skyline drawing, in its moulded panel above the mantel. Earlier mockups replaced it with a gilt oil painting. **It stays**, and the room is composed around it.

The key moves:

- **One strong Arts & Crafts rug** in the style of Morris's Hammersmith rugs. It is the room's main colour and pattern, and it is clearly different from the geometric hall runner and from the plain modern rug in the TV area.
- **Madder red** cushions on the olive velvet chairs. Madder was Morris's favourite dye, and this is the room's terracotta thread. It is picked up by the iron red in two pieces of Imari porcelain.
- **Dark antique bronze** for the metal: the reading lamp and the green-glass banker's lamp on the piano.
- **A real library on the shelves**, with the family photos kept, and one antique-style local map above the piano.
- **Clutter out:** the two dated uplighters, the floating pedestal and the hamper on the hearth.

**Earlier user preferences this design keeps:**
- no painting
- the green armchairs, the piano, the desk with two monitors and the task chair all stay
- the room stays a usable office
- a large rug that anchors both chairs but leaves a **modest oak gap (about 20–25 cm) before the black glazed rear screen**
- shelves with more than books
- traditional art, not modern

**A deliberate change from earlier explorations.** The earlier reception mockups used a brown Persian-style rug. The hall runner is now the house's brown Persian-character rug, and the rule is that no rug style repeats. The reception therefore gets an Arts & Crafts design instead. View 3 shows the same rug design on a **warm wheat ground** as a comparison, closer to the lighter brown the user was weighing up.
