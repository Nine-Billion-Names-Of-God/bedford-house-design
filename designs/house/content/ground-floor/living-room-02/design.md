# Living Room · Mockup 02 · Collected, Not Matching

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status: revised proposal for review.** The user rejected the modern floating cabinet, felt that similar rugs and styling were making the rooms look too alike, and asked for an older look, more character, interesting pieces and proper consideration of the ornaments already on the shelf.

This version removes the floating cabinet, trials a low aged-oak coffer, changes to a more colourful geometric kilim-style rug and a turned-leg table, restores the existing clock and bases the shelf on the user's actual collection. The wall-mounted TV instruction remains. No painting is proposed.

Both opposite views are saved as a new iteration. Living Room 01 and every earlier room's images and notes remain unchanged. The [live brief](/house/ground-floor/brief) records this feedback as a wider design direction: related rooms can have distinct rugs, furniture and personal objects.

## 1. Why the earlier version missed the mark

The repeated pale floral rugs, walnut forms and generic pottery imposed a similar formula on several rooms. In this room that also displaced the user's actual ornaments. That was an over-edit, not a judgement that the original pieces were unsuitable.

The revised starting point is the existing collection: the red hat and glass piece, decorated plate, darker figures, small animals and copper-coloured pots already give the room particular character. New furniture and a rug should make a setting for those pieces rather than replace them with an anonymous display.

The aim remains curated and elegant. Here that means giving the collection deliberate positions and leaving useful surfaces clear, not removing everything that has a different colour or shape.

## 2. TV-wall view: older furniture and the actual shelf collection

**Image:** `tv-wall.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-02/tv-wall.png)

### A low oak coffer, not a modern media unit

The TV remains mounted on its existing wall. The floating cabinet is removed completely. Beneath it, a low antique-style oak coffer/blanket chest introduces patina, visible grain, panel detail and restrained iron hardware. It sits on the floor as a separate piece of furniture rather than extending across the wall like fitted joinery.

This is a style and proportion study, not an image of a sourced antique. An initial envelope around 130–140 cm wide × 35–40 cm deep × 45–48 cm high is worth testing against the actual screen, wall and route. The render is more decorative/carved than the simple panelled chest originally envisaged; a plainer genuinely older piece could be preferable to a new heavily distressed imitation.

The chest is not required to support the screen or to function as a purpose-built TV unit. Check whether it is wanted for storage, and how actual devices and cables would be arranged. Do not assume its closed interior provides suitable device ventilation or cable access. Its deeper footprint compared with the rejected floating unit needs to be included in the route check.

The top stays almost clear. There is no need to add a plant and a set of matching ornaments just because it is a surface.

## 3. View towards the kitchen: rug, table and clock

**Image:** `towards-kitchen.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-02/towards-kitchen.png)

### A different rug language

The floral brown/Persian-style rug is replaced with a kilim-style geometric design in softened brick/terracotta, ochre/flax and slate/indigo blue, with small olive notes. Its larger stepped diamonds and lozenges bring pattern at a different scale from the rugs explored in the reception and hall.

This gives the room an independent character while sharing colours with the user's hat, rust textiles, green plants and oak floor. It does not require every room to have the same rug pattern. The neutral taupe sofa provides a quieter setting for this more expressive textile.

The image is a kilim-style concept, not an authenticated textile or a claim of regional origin, age, dye or weaving method. The rendered surface can read more pile-like than the flatwoven texture intended. A real candidate should be judged for its colour, surface and comfort in this room rather than selected solely because it carries a particular label.

The rug remains large enough to anchor the sofa and table, with oak around the edges, space before the steel doorway and a clear end before the kitchen. Its revised pattern is not an instruction to make the footprint larger. Check the actual route, sofa feet and door operation with a floor template.

### A table with an older profile

A small dark-stained tray-top table with turned legs and low stretchers replaces the repeated oval walnut form. It brings a different silhouette and finish from the coffer, rather than creating a matching suite. Start around 70–75 × 40–45 cm if it fits the seating and route.

Its top stays useful: a remote or the existing small dish is enough. The table is a visual concept; no specific antique, maker, date or available listing is represented.

### Bring the clock back

The user's original round clock returns above the sofa, within the existing wall panelling. The invented landscape painting from 01 is removed. The clock already belongs to the room and adds a different kind of wall feature from the traditional paintings explored elsewhere.

It is not necessary to replace an existing useful piece with a painting simply to make a room look designed. No painting of the wall or panelling is involved.

## 4. What stays from 01

- The mounted TV, in its original wall position and at a seated-viewing height to be set from the actual sofa.
- Straight warm taupe sofa, rather than a chaise, with the existing proposed rust/olive/flax cushion grouping and a single optional throw.
- Small marble/metal side table and brass/linen lamp at the reception-door end of the sofa, subject to door clearance.
- Existing monstera at the kitchen side of the shelf/TV wall, without replacing the shelf ornaments with trailing plants.
- Actual raised grey dog bed trialled at the kitchen end of the sofa; retain its real footprint and revise the layout if it cannot fit.
- All wall, ceiling and joinery colours, floor, mouldings, windows, kitchen/dining fabric and black steel glazing.

These are carried forward for continuity; none of the new furniture, rug or layout choices have been recorded as user-approved purchases.

## 5. Distinction between rooms

The overall house can share warm timber, a considered amount of rust, green, brass and natural textiles. Those connections do not require identical furniture shapes, pale brown floral rugs or the same ceramic group in every room.

For this room, the character comes from **the existing collection, a geometric textile and older furniture profiles**. The reception's art-led reading/study scheme and the hall's quieter palette remain separate explorations. No other room has been rewritten by this iteration; the feedback should inform future revisions when those rooms are revisited.

## 6. Fit and practical checks

Keep the route from reception to kitchen usable with the coffer, table and dog bed in place. The coffer's depth and any lid movement need checking beneath the mounted TV. Final screen height, mount position, power and device/cable provision remain unresolved by the render.

Use the actual shelf, ornaments, clock and hat to establish scale; do not buy substitutes based on generated shapes. Check rug backing/underlay against the floor and any underfloor heating. Sofa, rug and table sizes are starting proportions, not measured clearances.

The coffer and table are not sourced products or proof that an appropriately sized old piece is available. Once the direction is agreed, seek individual pieces with real dimensions and condition information, rather than specifying a generic matched antique-style set.
