# Living Room · Mockup 01 · Warm & Personal

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status: first TV/living-area proposal for review.** This is the middle room between the glazed front reception and the open kitchen, not the front room with the piano. The user confirmed the TV will be wall-mounted and the floor-standing TV stand is not required. They invited suggestions for anything beneath the TV, the existing ladder shelf, the red Vietnamese hat, a rug, a different sofa, plants and lighting.

The proposal replaces the large chaise with a straight taupe sofa, adds a quietly patterned rug and small oval table, retains the ladder shelf with an edited display and gives the red hat a deliberate position above it. A shallow floating walnut cabinet beneath the mounted TV provides optional concealed storage without returning a large unit to the floor.

No painting or architectural work is proposed beyond the authorised TV mounting and the proposed furniture/art fixings. All entrance/hall and reception-room iterations remain unchanged. The [live brief](/house/ground-floor/brief) remains the whole-project record.

## 2. Overall arrangement

Keep the sofa and TV on their current opposite walls. The principal space gain comes from removing the chaise projection and the large floor-standing black TV unit. A straight sofa, shallow floating storage and a small round-ended table allow the seating area to remain defined without occupying the full route through the house.

The rug links the sofa and table, with visible oak around it and a clear end before the kitchen. Keep the reception's glazed doorway and door leaves clear. Exact edges need a floor template rather than trusting the apparent clearances in the images.

The room is the hinge between the period reception and contemporary kitchen. Simple sofa/cabinet forms relate to the rear, while walnut, aged brass, a quiet traditional rug and landscape art connect it to the front. Small cinnamon/rust accents keep the shared colour thread without making the whole room red.

## 3. TV wall, floating storage and ladder shelf

**Image:** `tv-wall.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-01/tv-wall.png)

### Wall-mounted TV

Mount the existing TV on its current wall opposite the sofa. The intention is a low, comfortable seated-viewing position, not a screen pushed up towards the cornice. Determine the actual position while seated on the selected sofa; the render is not an installation height specification.

The existing socket positions remain visible in the image. Final mount position and cable/device routing are not solved by image generation. Plan those around the actual wall and power provision without assuming permission to repaint the room.

### Why propose something underneath?

A shallow floating cabinet provides a quiet place for media devices, remotes and small everyday items. Its plain dark-walnut front relates to the coffee table and the kitchen's darker finishes, while visible floor beneath keeps it lighter than the current unit.

An initial proportion to test is around 140–150 cm wide × 25–30 cm deep × 22–25 cm high. The rendered cabinet can read larger than that; measure the actual screen, wall and device requirements before choosing it. Keep the top largely clear, rather than turning it into another crowded display surface.

This is optional. If devices and cables can be managed neatly without storage, the TV can remain on an otherwise clear wall. The cabinet is proposed for function and proportion, not because a mounted TV must have furniture beneath it.

### Shelf styling: use the existing piece

Retain the black leaning shelf with its timber shelves. Its frame already relates to the glazed doors; replacing it is not necessary to make the room work.

| Shelf level | Starting arrangement | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top | One small ceramic object or compact plant, with open space | Keeps the hat above as the focal piece, rather than competing with a crowded top. |
| Next | One small meaningful framed photograph and a low vessel | Gives personal objects space to read. |
| Middle | A shallow bowl beside a short book group | Mixes shapes and retains usable books. |
| Lower display | Two or three selected existing ornaments, grouped rather than evenly dotted across the shelf | Keeps the character of the user's collection without making every item compete. |
| Lowest | The main practical book group | Gives the display a grounded base and keeps books easy to retrieve. |

The actual ornaments have not been inventoried. Select the pieces that matter most and rotate some if needed; do not dispose of items or replace them all with matching new ceramics. The generated objects are illustrative, not purchase instructions.

## 4. The red Vietnamese hat

**Recommendation: keep it here, but move it above the ladder shelf.** It gives the room a personal note and a distinctive shape that a generic painting would not replace. Its deep red relates to the rust/cinnamon accents already used elsewhere on the ground floor.

The current position beside the TV and steel screen makes it look rather isolated. Centring it over the shelf turns the hat and objects into one composition. Use the existing hat, not a decorative substitute, and leave space between it, the shelf frame and the cornice. The image's apparent height is a proportional study; check the real shelf and hat dimensions before fixing it.

Keep it as the single strongest red object on this wall. The sofa cushions are muted and the rug quiet, so the hat has a clear role rather than joining a collection of competing red accents.

If the user ultimately prefers a calmer TV wall, the hat could be considered with personal objects in the dining area during that room's design. That is an alternative to assess, not a confirmed relocation. There is no need to remove it from the living room simply to achieve an elegant scheme.

## 5. Sofa wall and view towards the kitchen

**Image:** `towards-kitchen.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-01/towards-kitchen.png)

The sofa is a straight three-seater in medium warm mushroom/putty taupe, with slim arms, short timber legs and a tactile woven fabric. It is deliberately deeper in tone than the existing cream leather and has no chaise. Start by testing around 220–225 cm length and 90–95 cm depth, adjusted to the actual wall, door and dog-bed arrangement.

Use three cushions in total: one muted cinnamon/rust, one moss/olive and one flax lumbar with a fine tobacco stripe. A single neatly placed oatmeal throw is optional. The purpose is to connect the rooms, not to pile textiles over the seats.

The large clock is trial-replaced by one traditional horizontal landscape, in olive, umber and soft sky tones, in a restrained dark frame with a fine aged-gold detail. It sits within the existing wall moulding. The clock is retained as a possession; storing it or considering another location requires discussion, not disposal.

The existing small marble/metal side table is proposed at the reception-door end of the sofa, with a small aged-brass/flax-shade lamp. That gives a local evening glow without adding a floor lamp beside the dog bed or in the route. Confirm that the table and lamp clear the glazed door.

## 6. Rug, coffee table and plants

The rug is a matte, low-pile, softly faded traditional design in light mushroom/stone brown, with quiet brown/olive detail. It should sit comfortably between the reception's traditional direction and the simpler kitchen finishes. A starting template around 200 × 240–250 cm may be useful, but its exact width/length must come from the route, sofa and doorway positions.

Place the sofa's front feet on it and stop the edges short of the steel doorway and kitchen zone. The rug should define the sitting area, not cover the entire floor. Confirm backing/underlay compatibility with the actual floor and any underfloor heating.

The small low oval walnut table introduces a softer shape between the straight sofa and TV cabinet. Start around 75 × 45 cm with a low profile, then check reach and walking space. Its top needs only a dish or remote, not a book stack, flowers and candles. Exact table shape and size vary between the two renders.

Retain the existing monstera as the principal floor plant, tucked near the kitchen side of the shelf/TV wall with leaves clear of the TV and route. The images also show small trailing plants on the shelf/cabinet; these are optional, and fewer may be better with the actual ornaments. Keep watering and any trailing growth clear of electronics. No extra large plant is needed beside the sofa.

## 7. Dog bed and practical use

Keep the actual raised grey dog bed rather than staging it out of the room. Trial it at the kitchen end of the sofa, against the same wall, so it occupies the sofa-side strip rather than the centre of the route. The reverse image shows it partly cropped in the foreground; the other view does not clearly show it.

Its actual footprint, roughly 90 × 60 cm as an initial estimate, must be checked with the selected sofa. Do not shrink it to make the layout appear to fit or replace it with a small decorative basket. If the sofa and bed cannot share that wall length while maintaining access, revise the sofa dimensions or assess another location in a new iteration. The feeding position remains an open whole-floor question.

The coffee table should sit towards the sofa rather than in the TV-side walking line. Test the route with the dog bed in use, doors open and someone seated; the images do not prove a measured clearance.
