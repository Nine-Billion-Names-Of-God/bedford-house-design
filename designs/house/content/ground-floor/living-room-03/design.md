# Living Room · Mockup 03 · Sofa Colour

Date: 2026-10-25

**Status: three colour trials for review; no selection made.** The user asked whether a new sofa colour could give this room its own identity rather than continuing a beige palette everywhere. These images explore petrol blue, deep olive and oxblood in the same room view.

The base is the kitchen-facing view from [Living Room 02](/house/ground-floor/living-room-02/design). The proposal is a colour comparison, not a new sofa shape, room arrangement or round of accessory changes. All earlier images and notes remain unchanged.

## 1. A · Petrol blue

**Image:** `a-petrol-blue.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-03/a-petrol-blue.png)

A medium-deep, muted blue with a subtle green undertone. It picks up the slate/indigo in the kilim while giving the large seating piece a clearer colour of its own. The oak coffer, warm floor and brick/rust notes keep the combination from feeling cold.

This is the designer's preferred direction for the user's stated aim of making the rooms feel distinct. The front reception already has green armchairs; a blue sofa gives the living area a different dominant upholstery colour while still sharing colours from the rug and objects.

Aim for a muted, substantial blue rather than bright turquoise, denim or a near-black navy. The image is an artistic colour target, not a named fabric or calibrated swatch.

## 2. B · Deep olive

**Image:** `b-deep-olive.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-03/b-deep-olive.png)

A darker earthy green with a tobacco undertone. It relates naturally to the plant, warmer rug details and older timber, creating a grounded, collected mood. The lighter flax cushion and throw keep some contrast on the sofa.

Olive is a strong alternative to beige, but it also repeats the green family already present in the front room. Choose it if that relationship is desirable; petrol blue creates a clearer change between the two seating rooms.

The intended green should remain visibly olive rather than becoming brown or almost black under the evening lamps. Judge a real fabric sample in both daylight and lamp light.

## 3. C · Oxblood

**Image:** `c-oxblood.png` · [Open full image](/design-assets/house/ground-floor/living-room-03/c-oxblood.png)

A muted wine-red with a brown undertone. It creates the warmest and richest direction of the three and has a natural relationship to the red glass piece, Vietnamese hat and brick colours in the kilim.

Because it repeats those red notes on a much larger surface, it produces a more enveloping colour scheme. The blue and olive offer different relationships to the same rug. All are options for review, not approvals of a fixed palette.

Keep the direction deep and earthy rather than scarlet, pink or purple. The image shows a matte textile, not a recommendation for shiny red leather or a different sofa style.

## 4. Comparison

| Direction | Character in this room | Relationship to the rest of the house |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Petrol blue | A distinct cool colour balanced by warm wood and brick notes | Introduces a dominant upholstery colour not already used by the front-room chairs |
| Deep olive | Grounded and earthy | Connects most closely to the reception's green chairs and existing foliage |
| Oxblood | Warm, rich and enveloping | Enlarges the shared rust/red thread and relates to the user's red objects |

The recommendation is **petrol blue as the first direction to test in fabric**. That is the designer's judgement; the user has not selected a colour or product.

## 5. What is held constant

The intended comparison changes the main sofa upholstery only: body, seat and back cushions, arms and visible fabric surfaces. Its straight three-seat form, size, timber legs and position are retained.

The rust, olive and flax decorative cushions and oatmeal throw remain in place across all three images. They are held constant so the sofa colour can be judged; after a colour is chosen, a restrained cushion adjustment can be considered in a new version if useful.

The geometric kilim-style rug, older oak coffer, turned-leg table, original clock, shelf with actual ornaments/books, red hat, monstera, lamps, kitchen and room layout are carried forward from 02. No wall, ceiling or joinery painting, new furniture or architectural change is proposed here. The dog-bed fit and TV mounting/device provision remain open practical items from the room proposal.

Using the current room as the setting does not record every item in it as user-approved. The sofa remains a proposed replacement for the original leather chaise; no fabric, manufacturer or final size has been selected.

## 6. Generation and visual review

All three images were made with the **built-in image-generation tool**, one call per colour, using `living-room-02/towards-kitchen.png` as the same edit target. Exact prompts and filenames are saved in `generation-prompts.json` alongside these notes.

The prompts requested the same shape, weave, framing, lighting and background, changing only upholstery colour. Visual review confirms three clearly different sofa colours in the same room setting. Generated fabric texture and small details may vary, and the texture reads more pronounced in some versions. These are colour studies, not proof of an identical physical weave or a precisely controlled photographic recolour.

The illustrations do not establish a fabric colour code, lightfastness, cleaning performance, durability or product availability. A real sample in the room is needed before selection, alongside checking the actual sofa and dog-bed footprint. Previous iteration files are preserved without replacement.

## 7. Next decision

Choose the colour direction first. Then compare actual upholstery samples with the rug, wood, clock, hat and existing objects in the room's daylight and evening light. Keep further changes focused so the colour decision does not get lost in a different layout or a new set of accessories. Any revised mockups receive a new iteration.
